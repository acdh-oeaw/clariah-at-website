Implicitly Abusive Language:\
Data, Biases and Challenges in Detection
----------------------------------------

with Michael Wiegand, Senior Scientist in Digital Philology at the Faculty of Philological and Cultural Studies at the [University of Vienna](https://homepage.univie.ac.at/michael.wiegand/).

**When:** Tuesday, October 20th 2026; 15:45 - 17:45

**Where:** Austrian Academy of Sciences\
Baeckerstrasse 13, 1010 Vienna, Austria\
Seminarraum 1 (accessible via the courtyard)

**Registration:** Will open soon.

**Presentation Language:** English

---

Implicitly abusive language refers to expressions that convey derogatory, offensive or harmful meaning without relying on overtly abusive words. Instead, abuse may be communicated indirectly through stereotypes, pragmatic inference, contextual assumptions or subtle forms of othering.

This talk examines why such language remains difficult to model in abusive language detection, focusing on challenges related to data, dataset biases and classification. It discusses why existing datasets often contain detrimental biases, such as identity-group bias, and argues for the need for more focused datasets targeting specific subproblems in implicit abuse detection.

The talk also considers how crowdsourcing and large language models can contribute to the creation and analysis of such datasets. Finally, it addresses the difficulty of modelling the perceived abusiveness of non-negative stereotypes, whose interpretation may depend on semantic variation, generalization, perspective framing and the identity group being described.
